# Manipulation Validation Codebook

The 135-item stratified validation sample is evaluated using the same four 1–5 ordinal dimensions by the two blinded human reviewers and by the supplementary blinded Claude Sonnet 5.5 evaluation. The intended communicative-orientation condition is not shown during rating.

## 1. Experiential orientation

Rate the extent to which the transformed message communicates the problem through lived experience and is organized around feelings, intuitions, impressions, immediate reactions, subjective uncertainty, personal salience, and the way concerns arise in the user's experience. Facts and practical considerations may still be present, but they are less likely to organize the message through explicit criteria, alternatives, pros and cons, consequences, or structured tradeoffs. Rate experiential orientation independently of analytical orientation.

- **1 — Very low:** Lived experience, feelings, intuitions, impressions, and subjective concerns play little or no role in organizing the message.
- **2 — Low:** Some experiential material is present, but it is mostly peripheral rather than an organizing feature.
- **3 — Moderate:** Experiential material is clearly present and contributes to how the problem is communicated, but does not strongly organize the message.
- **4 — High:** Lived experience, feelings, intuitions, impressions, or subjective concerns clearly organize substantial parts of the message.
- **5 — Very high:** The message is strongly organized around the user's lived and subjective experience throughout.

## 2. Analytical orientation

Rate the extent to which the transformed message communicates the problem through explicit deliberation and is organized around relevant facts, constraints, priorities, alternatives, consequences, uncertainties, and tradeoffs. A message higher in analytical orientation tends to make relationships among relevant considerations explicit and may explicitly contrast or weigh competing considerations. Feelings and subjective experiences may still be present. Rate analytical orientation independently of experiential orientation.

- **1 — Very low:** Explicit deliberation or structured consideration of relevant factors, alternatives, consequences, or tradeoffs is largely absent.
- **2 — Low:** Some analytical consideration is present, but it is mostly peripheral rather than an organizing feature.
- **3 — Moderate:** Explicit deliberation is clearly present and contributes to how the problem is communicated, but does not strongly organize the message.
- **4 — High:** Explicit consideration of relevant facts, constraints, priorities, alternatives, consequences, uncertainty, or tradeoffs clearly organizes substantial parts of the message.
- **5 — Very high:** The message is strongly organized around explicit deliberation and relationships among relevant considerations throughout.

## 3. Naturalness / plausibility

Rate the extent to which the transformed message could plausibly have been written by a real person seeking advice in an online conversational setting. Do not penalize a message simply because it is more emotionally expressive, more structured, or organized differently from the source. Judge whether it remains plausible as human advice-seeking communication.

- **1 — Very low:** Clearly unnatural, artificial, or implausible as real advice-seeking communication.
- **2 — Low:** Contains substantial unnatural or constructed features that make it difficult to imagine as a real advice-seeking message.
- **3 — Acceptable:** Generally plausible, although some wording or organization may feel somewhat constructed.
- **4 — High:** Reads naturally and plausibly as something a person could realistically write when seeking advice.
- **5 — Very high:** Reads highly naturally and convincingly as authentic advice-seeking communication.

## 4. Decision-situation fidelity

Rate the extent to which the transformed message represents the **same underlying advice-seeking or decision-making situation** as the source. Focus on preservation of the underlying problem, relevant considerations and constraints, uncertainty or stance, and advice-seeking goal. Exact wording, sentence structure, organization, and one-to-one preservation of every minor detail are not required. Reorganization, condensation, or changes in emphasis are acceptable when they do not materially alter the underlying decision problem.

- **1 — Very low:** Represents a substantially different decision situation, problem, stance, or advice-seeking goal.
- **2 — Low:** Important changes substantially alter how the underlying decision or advice-seeking problem should be understood.
- **3 — Acceptable:** Some details, emphasis, or organization differ, but the same underlying decision situation and advice-seeking problem are preserved sufficiently to be treated as the same scenario.
- **4 — High:** The same underlying decision situation, relevant considerations, uncertainty or stance, and advice-seeking goal are clearly preserved, with only minor non-substantive differences.
- **5 — Very high:** The underlying decision situation, relevant considerations, uncertainty or stance, and advice-seeking goal are preserved very closely despite the change in communicative orientation.

## Important rating rules

- Evaluate each dimension independently.
- Do not infer what score is expected or attempt to identify the intended experimental condition.
- Do not reward polished or formal writing simply because it appears more sophisticated.
- Experiential orientation does not imply irrationality, lower intelligence, greater distress, impulsivity, or confusion.
- Analytical orientation does not imply greater intelligence, sophistication, certainty, decisiveness, or correctness.
- Experiential and analytical orientation are not opposite ends of a single scale. A message can be high in both or low in both.
- For naturalness, judge plausibility as real advice-seeking communication rather than similarity to the source's writing style.
- For decision-situation fidelity, focus on preservation of the substantive decision or advice-seeking situation rather than exact wording or one-to-one preservation of every detail.

## Manipulation-check interpretation criteria

The rating scales above are applied independently and without revealing the intended condition to raters. After ratings are collected, the following operational criteria are used to evaluate whether each transformed stimulus exhibits the intended communicative-orientation profile:

- **Experiential condition:** experiential orientation **>= 4** and analytical orientation **<= 2**.
- **Analytical condition:** analytical orientation **>= 4** and experiential orientation **<= 2**.
- **Integrated condition:** experiential orientation **>= 3** and analytical orientation **>= 3**, with the two orientation ratings differing by **no more than 1 scale point**.
- **Naturalness / plausibility:** a rating of **>= 3** indicates that the transformed message is at least generally plausible as naturally occurring advice-seeking communication.
- **Decision-situation fidelity:** a rating of **>= 3** indicates that the underlying decision or advice-seeking situation is sufficiently preserved to be treated as the same substantive scenario.

These criteria are used for interpretation of the manipulation check after blinded rating; they are not shown as expected condition-specific targets in the reviewer rating materials.

## Reliability

Intercoder reliability for the two human reviewers is calculated separately for each of the four 1–5 ordinal dimensions using ordinal Krippendorff's alpha from the independent pre-adjudication ratings.
